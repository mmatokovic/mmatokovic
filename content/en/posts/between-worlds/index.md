To enter the spirit world, a shaman has to initiate trance through the hunting of a tutelary spirit or power animal. The eland often serves as the power animal. Its fat is used symbolically in many rituals, including initiations and rites of passage. Other animals, such as the giraffe, kudu and hartebeest, can serve the same function.

One of the most important rituals in the San religion is the great dance, or trance dance. The dance typically takes a circular form, with women clapping and singing and men dancing rhythmically. Although there is no evidence that the Kalahari San use hallucinogens regularly, a shaman in training may use them to enter trance for the first time.

Psychologists have studied hallucinations and altered states of consciousness in the field of neuropsychology. They found that entoptic phenomena can be brought on by rhythmic dancing, music, sensory deprivation, hyperventilation, prolonged and intense concentration, and migraines. The psychological approach explains rock art through three phases of trance. In the first phase, an altered state of consciousness sets in, and people see geometric shapes known as entoptic phenomena: zigzags, chevrons, dots, flecks, grids, vortices and U-shapes. These shapes appear especially often in the rock engravings of southern Africa.

During the second phase of trance, people try to make sense of the entoptic phenomena. They elaborate the shapes they have "seen" until they resemble something familiar. Shamans in the second phase bring the natural world into these visions, seeing honeycombs or other familiar forms.
